“during, before, after” + a noun is a phrase; only a subject + finite verb makes a clause.

  • A. Comma splice: the phrase would attach to the first IC, then “, its thinkers merged” joins a second IC with a comma alone.

Incorrect · Correct answer: A

“whereas” = in contrast: comma before it, nothing after it.

  • A. Correct: “fluctuations, whereas in dry summer months, it helps …”: comma before the contrast DC.
  • B. A comma right after “whereas” cuts the conjunction off from its own clause.
  • C. A semicolon needs an IC after it; “whereas …” is a DC.
  • D. Fragment: “Whereas in dry summer months, it helps ….” would stand alone as a DC.

Question 9 · Difficult

Angelina Weld Grimké published dozens of poems in many leading journals in the early 20th century, receiving widespread praise for her lyrical explorations of racial identity. Her sole full-length drama, Rachel, was performed only a handful of times, ______ has since been revived in several notable productions and adaptations.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Incorrect · Correct answer: B

Show explanation

“however” joins nothing: before a second IC you need a period or a semicolon.

  • A. “however but” piles two contrast words together, and “but” has no comma before it.
  • B. Correct: “however” ends the first IC; a period starts the second.
  • C. Run-on: “however it” leaves two ICs with no mark between them.
  • D. Comma splice: “however” cannot join two ICs with commas.

Find the second subject + finite verb (“it would inspire”): if the part before the blank is already an IC, you need a period.

  • A. Comma splice: the phrase would hang on the first IC, then “, it would later inspire” joins a second IC with a comma alone.
