The Comma (continued)

  • Three marks, one job. Paired commas, paired dashes and parentheses all set off extra information. Parentheses are usually a wrong choice on the SAT.
  • A general noun needs its clause. Researchers alone does not say which researchers, so who work at night is core. Most job and group words work this way.
  • ✓ Students who sleep late miss the first bus.
  • ✗ Students, who sleep late, miss the first bus. (this now says all students sleep late)
  • Name first or label first? After a name, the label is extra. After a general label, the name is core. A noun that already points to one thing works like a name: only one object can be the museum’s oldest.
  • ✓ Aluminum oxide, a chemical compound, has many uses.
  • ✓ The chemical compound aluminum oxide has many uses.
  • ✗ The chemical compound, aluminum oxide, has many uses.
  • ✓ The museum’s oldest object, a bronze mirror, stands near the entrance.
  • Three word families. Name the family first. It tells you the punctuation.

  • though can be in two families. Before a clause it makes a DC. Set off inside one IC, it works like however.
  • ✓ Though the trial failed, the team continued.
  • ✓ The second trial, though, was costly.
  • Delete however with both its commas. What is left must still be correct.
  • ✓ The second method, however, required more time. → The second method required more time.
  • ✗ The trial failed, however, the team continued. → The trial failed the team continued. (two ICs with nothing between them)
  • that can be left out. After note, report, summarize or write, the object is often a that clause, with or without that. The clause is one unit, so no comma goes after the verb.
  • ✓ Critics have noted [that] the play compares two generations.
  • ✓ The author writes [that] the town changed slowly.
  • Question 10. the way is the object of noted, and a that is left out after it: the way [that] playwright Wakako Yamauchi compares …. Inside that clause, the subject is playwright Wakako Yamauchi and the verb is compares, so no comma goes between them. pioneering is an adjective, so a noun must follow it.
  • Question 11. as a natural and effective way has no verb, and it continues using a species: using a species as a way. the Boer goat only sits in between, so it is extra.
  • Question 12. A semicolon needs an IC on both sides. because cheesemakers have long relied … is a DC, so every semicolon choice is out.
  • how, what and why clauses can be objects. Count the main verbs. One main verb means one IC, so there is no comma before and.
  • ✓ We studied how bees navigate and why they return. (one main verb: studied)
  • ✗ We studied how bees navigate, and why they return.
  • ✓ We studied how bees navigate, and we tested why they return. (two main verbs: two ICs)
  • List commas go between items, never after the last one (Question 16).
  • ✓ The exhibit was so bright, so loud, and so crowded that we left early.
  • ✗ The exhibit was so bright, so loud, and so crowded, that we left early.
  • Why the SAT writes A, B, and C. A, B and C can read as A plus one unit, B and C. Other writing may accept it, but use the final comma here.
  • Long subject? Peel it. Cross out each prepositional phrase until one noun is left. That noun also decides the verb form.
  • The rapidly expanding body [of research] [on how slow-wave sleep stabilizes newly formed memories] [through coordinated neural reactivation] [in the hippocampus and neocortex] underscores …

From Data to a Defensible Claim

  • Tag the lines. Write short labels on the graph (FC, FS, MC, MS), so you do not keep going back to the key.
  • Group the lines two ways and say what you see. By treatment: FC vs MC, FS vs MS. By sex: FC + FS, MC + MS. Here the female lines move far apart, and the male lines stay closer together.
  • Match a new term to the graph. Neuron activation is not on the graph. The text says CNO activates the neurons, so the response is CNO compared with saline, for each sex.
  • Check the claim before the numbers. The claim is about a difference between females and males. Choice A never compares them, so it is out before you read the graph. Choice B is true, but it covers saline only, so it shows no response.
  • Say exactly what the data show. Write “females were more active”, not “a more positive effect”.
