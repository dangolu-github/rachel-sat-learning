4. Core and Extra

  • C9Extra information takes a mark at both ends.
    • In the middle of a sentence: two commas. At the end: one comma before it.
    • Never leave the opening comma unclosed: it would cut the subject off from its verb.
    • The findings, according to the report, support the new model.
    • Jane Okafor, a biologist at the institute led the trial.
    • Jane Okafor, a biologist at the institute, led the trial.
    • We consulted Dr. Chen, who led the first trial.
  • C10An adjective clause takes commas only when it is extra.
    • Extra — it adds a fact about a noun already identified: commas at both ends.
    • Core — it tells which one is meant: no commas.
    • that opens only a core clause; which and who can open either kind.
    • Lift the clause out: is the same person or thing still identified? Yes → extra; no → core.
    • Check: splitting off an extra clause preserves the reference; splitting off a core clause changes the intended restriction.
    • The reports, that contain maps, are on the upper shelf.
    • The reports, which contain maps, are on the upper shelf.
    • The reports that contain maps are on the upper shelf.
    • Researchers who work at night reported more errors.

SAT Questions

Question 7 · C9
Santa Clara Pueblo artist Roxanne Swentzell’s sculpture Mud Woman Rolls On consists of five human figures made of clay and plant fiber and arranged in descending size; each figure holds the smaller one in front of it. The arrangement of the figures, according to ______ represents her idea that “we all come from the Earth, generation after generation.”

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. Swentzell
  2. Swentzell,
  3. Swentzell:
  4. Swentzell—
Worked Answer

B. Swentzell,

  1. Core: “The arrangement of the figures … represents her idea …”.
  2. “according to Swentzell” is extra information in the middle, and a comma already stands before “according”.
  3. Extra information in the middle needs a mark at both ends → B.
Question 8 · C9
The extraordinary survival story of Ada Blackjack, an Iñupiat seamstress who was stranded for two years on Russia’s remote Wrangel Island after the disastrous 1921 Canadian Arctic ______ the fortitude and resourcefulness required to endure one of the harshest environments on Earth.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. Expedition highlights
  2. Expedition, highlights
  3. Expedition but highlights
  4. Expedition; highlights
Worked Answer

B. Expedition, highlights

  1. Core: subject “The extraordinary survival story of Ada Blackjack”, verb “highlights”.
  2. The comma after “Blackjack” opens an appositive about her: “an Iñupiat seamstress who was stranded … after the disastrous 1921 Canadian Arctic Expedition”.
  3. The appositive is extra information in the middle of the sentence, so a second comma closes it before the verb → B.
Question 9 · C10
When particles are suspended in liquid (like pollen in a water glass), they will zigzag randomly through the liquid and collide with one another in perpetuity. This type of random, continuous ______ is known as Brownian motion, can be observed throughout the natural world.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. movement: which
  2. movement, which
  3. movement which
  4. movement. Which
Worked Answer

B. movement, which

  1. Core: “This type of random, continuous movement … can be observed throughout the natural world.”
  2. “which is known as Brownian motion” only adds a name to a movement already identified, and a comma already closes it after “motion”.
  3. An extra adjective clause needs a mark at both ends → B.

5. Names, Labels and Inserted Words
The idea · The same test, three more cases

1. Appositive

  • An appositive is a noun phrase placed beside another noun to rename it.
  • Commas or not? The lift-out test (4. Core and Extra) decides: core or extra.

2. Label or added name

  • A label before a name (biologist Jane Okafor) cannot say who is meant: the name is core.
  • A name or description after a noun that already identifies the thing (Jane Okafor, a biologist,) is extra.

3. Conjunctive adverbs

  • however, though, therefore, for example are conjunctive adverbs.
  • They show how ideas relate, but they are not conjunctions: they join nothing.
  • Count the ICs.
  • One IC: an adverb inserted in the middle takes a comma on each side. At the start or end, only one comma is needed. A DC does not change the IC count.
  • Two ICs: the adverb cannot join them. A semicolon or a period must (The Semicolon).
  • Biologist Jane Okaforsubject led the trial.
  • Jane Okaforsubject, a biologistextra, led the trial.
  • The second method, howeverextra, required more time.
  • The trial failedIC; however, the team continuedIC.
Test.
  1. Lift the part out, as in 4. Core and Extra.
  2. For however, count the ICs, then locate the adverb: middle of one IC → paired commas; two ICs → a semicolon or period at the clause boundary.
  • C11Label before a name → no commas.
    • The label cannot say who is meant, so the name is core.
    • Lift the name out and the sentence breaks.
    • Biologist Jane Okafor led the trial.
    • Biologist, Jane Okafor, led the trial.
    • The chemical compound aluminum oxide has many uses.
  • C12Name or description added after a noun that already identifies the thing → commas at both ends.
    • Lift it out: the sentence still works and means the same, so it is extra (C9).
    • Jane Okafor, a biologist, led the trial.
    • The museum’s oldest object, a bronze mirror, stands near the entrance.
  • C13However, though or for example inserted in the middle of one IC → a comma on each side.
    • At the start or end of the IC, use one comma to set it off. An attached DC does not change the IC count.
    • Between two ICs it cannot join (an adverb, not a conjunction): a comma there is a comma splice.
    • The second method, however, required more time.
    • The trial failed, however, the team continued.
    • The second trial, though, was costly.

SAT Questions

Question 10 · C11
The 1977 play And the Soul Shall Dance depicts two Japanese American farming families in Depression-era Southern California. Critics have noted the way pioneering ______ compares the experiences of issei (Japanese nationals who emigrated to America) and nisei (their American-born children).

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. playwright, Wakako Yamauchi,
  2. playwright, Wakako Yamauchi
  3. playwright Wakako Yamauchi,
  4. playwright Wakako Yamauchi
Worked Answer

D. playwright Wakako Yamauchi

  1. “pioneering playwright” is a label; the name “Wakako Yamauchi” says which playwright is meant.
  2. Lift the name out: “the way pioneering playwright compares …” no longer works, so the name is core.
  3. A label before a name takes no commas, and no comma separates the subject from its verb “compares” → D.
Question 11 · C12
In states with high fire danger, the use of goats for clearing brush has become an eco-friendly method to mitigate fire risk. Recently, the city of San Rafael in California implemented a program using a particularly voracious ______ as a natural and effective way to prepare wooded areas for the annual dry season.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. species the Boer goat,
  2. species, the Boer goat,
  3. species the Boer goat
  4. species, the Boer goat
Worked Answer

B. species, the Boer goat,

  1. “a particularly voracious species” already identifies the thing. Lift out “the Boer goat”: “using a particularly voracious species as a natural and effective way …” still works.
  2. So “the Boer goat” is extra information in the middle of the sentence.
  3. A mark at both ends → B.
Question 12 · C13
Pectin, a natural thickening agent found in many fruits, is added to many foods to achieve the desired consistency. It has not been used widely in cheesemaking ______ because cheesemakers have long relied on enzymes (like rennet) or acids (like vinegar) to achieve the desired consistency.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. recipes, though
  2. recipes, though,
  3. recipes, though;
  4. recipes; though,
Worked Answer

B. recipes, though,

  1. “though” here means “however”: a conjunctive adverb. Count the ICs: only one, “It has not been used widely in cheesemaking recipes”. The clause after it, “because cheesemakers have long relied on …”, is a DC attached to that IC, and a DC does not count.
  2. A conjunctive adverb inside the one IC is extra: a comma on each side → B.

6. Lists, and Places Where No Comma Goes

Lists

The idea · No reason, no comma

1. Every comma has a reason

  • between two ICs, with and / but / so …
  • at the border of an attached DC or phrase
  • at both ends of extra information
  • between three or more equal items in a list (this unit)

2. No reason, no comma

  • A single comma never separates core parts that belong together:
  • subject + verb · verb + object · preposition + object · so … that

3. Grammar, not pause

  • A comma marks grammar, not the pause you hear when reading aloud.
  • A long subject still takes no comma before its verb.
  • a compassitem 1, a mapitem 2, and a water filteritem 3
  • The results from both stationssubject suggestverb a changeobject.
Test.
  1. Before you put in a comma, name its reason.
  2. No reason → no comma.
  • C14Three or more equal items → a comma after each item before the last.
    • One kind of mark all through the list.
    • The SAT also puts a comma before the final and.
    • The kit contains a compass; a map, and a water filter.
    • The kit contains a compass, a map, and a water filter.
  • C15Two items joined by and → no comma, however long they are.
    • And already joins them.
    • When joining exactly two units, IC + IC takes a comma before and (C1); two shared-subject verbs or two items do not. Three-or-more-item lists follow C14.
    • The study shows how the virus spreads and what slows it.
    • The study shows how the virus spreads, and what slows it.

No Comma

  • C16No single comma between a subject and its verb, however long the subject is.
    • A comma right before the verb is correct only when it closes extra information (C9).
    • The results from both stations, suggest a change.
    • The results from both stations suggest a change.
  • C17No comma between a verb and its object, or before a that clause that completes the meaning.
    • After shows, the that clause is a noun clause (a DC used as a noun): the object.
    • After so …, the that clause completes so.
    • The researchers analyzed the samples.
    • The signal was so weak, that no one detected it.
    • The report shows that costs fell.
    • The signal was so weak that no one detected it.
  • C18No comma between a preposition and its object.
    • They form one prepositional phrase.
    • The tools were found in, a cave near the coast.
    • The tools were found in a cave near the coast.

SAT Questions

Question 15 · C16
The rapidly expanding body of research on how slow-wave sleep stabilizes newly formed memories through coordinated neural reactivation in the hippocampus and ______ the critical role of nightly rest in learning and has sparked fresh debate among cognitive scientists.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. neocortex underscores
  2. neocortex, underscores
  3. neocortex and underscores
  4. neocortex: underscores
Worked Answer

A. neocortex underscores

  1. Subject: “The rapidly expanding body of research on how slow-wave sleep stabilizes … in the hippocampus and neocortex”. Simple subject: “body”.
  2. Its verb is “underscores” (and, later, “has sparked”).
  3. No single comma between a subject and its verb, however long the subject is → A.
Question 16 · C17
Roughly 300 nights a year, when the cold air descending from the Andes Mountains meets the warm air rising from Venezuela’s coastal Lake Maracaibo, the result is a spectacular lightning storm, its strikes so bright, so localized, and so ______ that it has become known as “Maracaibo’s Lighthouse.”

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. dependable:
  2. dependable;
  3. dependable
  4. dependable,
Worked Answer

C. dependable

  1. “so bright, so localized, and so dependable” is a list of three; the blank comes after its last item.
  2. “that it has become known as …” completes “so … that”: it gives the result and cannot be cut off from “so dependable”.
  3. No mark before a that-clause that completes the meaning → C.
Question 17 · C18
After the printing press was introduced in 1440, handwritten manuscripts from Europe’s medieval period were often destroyed and the paper used for other purposes. In one instance, pages ______ a collection of Norse tales dating to 1270 were discovered lining a bishop’s miter (hat).

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  1. from:
  2. from,
  3. from
  4. from—
Worked Answer

C. from

  1. “from a collection of Norse tales dating to 1270” tells which pages: a preposition (“from”) and its object (“a collection …”).
  2. Nothing separates a preposition from its object → C.
