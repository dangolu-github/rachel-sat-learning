Sentence Boundaries · Class Notes

  • Find the main subject and its finite verb group before choosing punctuation.
  • Bracket phrases and relative clauses that modify the subject.
  • An independent clause has a complete subject–predicate core; a comma alone cannot join two independent clauses.

Apply 1 · A long subject is still one subject

Show answer and sentence core

B. The core is The natural and mechanical materials / highlight these two aspects of his background.

That Palma Rodríguez uses in his art modifies materials. The finite verb uses belongs to that relative clause; highlight is the main predicate. Do not place a comma between this long subject and highlight.

A separates the subject from its predicate. C and D incorrectly place a comma before the restrictive that-clause.

Show typed class notes and clarification
The natural mats that artist Palma uses in his art hightlight two aspects.

The natural mats, artist Palma uses in his art, hightlight two aspects.

Clarification: omitting the object relative pronoun that does not turn the clause into a parenthetical insertion. Keep the restrictive version unpunctuated: The natural materials the artist uses in his art highlight two aspects. A supplementary version needs which: The natural materials, which the artist uses in his art, highlight two aspects.

Try it 7 · Repair a comma splice

The first result was inconclusive, after checking the equipment, the researchers repeated the trial.

Show class annotations · Try it 7

Open full-size annotations

Show corrected sentence and reasoning

The first result was inconclusive; after checking the equipment, the researchers repeated the trial.

  • First core: the first result / was inconclusive.
  • Second core: the researchers / repeated the trial.
  • After checking the equipment belongs with the second core. The boundary comes before that opening phrase.

A finite verb can appear inside a dependent clause. An -ing form, infinitive, or participle does not automatically supply a main predicate. An appositive is a noun phrase that renames a noun; it is not any clause placed between commas.
