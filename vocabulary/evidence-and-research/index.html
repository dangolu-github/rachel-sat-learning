1. A reasonable possibility

conjecture / plausible

conjecture v. / n.
  • Meaning: Form an explanation from incomplete evidence; such an explanation.
  • Near-synonym: speculate
  • Meaning contrast: Establish means support or determine something firmly; conjecture proposes an explanation before that point.
  • The damaged inscription led the historian to conjecture that the vessel had belonged to a merchant, although its owner could not yet be identified.
  • Family: conjectural
plausible adj.
  • Meaning: Reasonable enough to be possible or believable.
  • Near-synonym: credible
  • Meaning contrast: Proven is stronger: a plausible explanation can be reasonable without being established.
  • A seasonal shortage was a plausible explanation for the price rise, but the surviving records did not establish its cause.
  • Family: plausibility; plausibly
Compare the meanings
  • A possibility can be plausible before it is established. Conjecturing proposes an explanation; it does not certify it.

2. Evidence that adds support

substantiate / corroborate

substantiate v.
  • Meaning: Supply evidence that gives a claim a firm basis.
  • Near-synonym: support with evidence
  • Meaning contrast: Merely assert means state a claim; substantiate means supply evidence for it.
  • Dated receipts substantiate the claim that the workshop exported cloth before 1850.
  • Family: substantiation
corroborate v.
  • Meaning: Support an account or result with further evidence that agrees with it.
  • Near-synonym: confirm; support
  • Meaning contrast: Contradict means provide conflicting evidence; corroborate adds evidence that agrees.
  • An independently kept diary corroborates the ship’s log by recording the same arrival date.
  • Family: corroboration
Compare the meanings
  • Substantiate stresses supplying a basis for a claim; corroborate often stresses agreement from further evidence. Their meanings overlap: use the whole context.

3. An objection and a disproof

rebut / refute

rebut v.
  • Meaning: Counter a claim with an argument or evidence.
  • Near-synonym: counter
  • Meaning contrast: Accept means agree with a claim; rebut means argue against it.
  • The reviewer rebutted the claim that the sample was representative by pointing out that it included only volunteers.
  • Family: rebuttal
refute v.
  • Meaning: Show that a claim is false through decisive reasoning or evidence.
  • Near-synonym: disprove
  • Meaning contrast: Substantiate supports a claim; refute, in the precise sense used here, establishes that it is false.
  • A dated photograph refuted the claim that the building had been demolished before 1920: it was still standing in 1931.
  • Family: refutation
Compare the meanings
  • Rebut stresses a counterargument; refute, in the precise sense used here, stresses successful disproof. A rebuttal does not automatically settle the issue.

4. A conclusion that earns confidence

scrutinize / cogent

scrutinize v.
  • Meaning: Examine closely and critically.
  • Near-synonym: examine closely
  • Meaning contrast: Skim means read quickly for an overview; scrutinize means examine closely and critically.
  • Before accepting the conclusion, the statistician scrutinized how missing responses had been handled.
  • Family: scrutiny
cogent adj.
  • Meaning: Clear, logically well supported and convincing.
  • Near-synonym: convincing; well reasoned
  • Meaning contrast: Incoherent means not logically connected; cogent describes reasoning whose connected points are convincing.
  • The essay’s cogent explanation connected the change in prices to the records of supply and addressed the strongest alternative account.
  • Family: cogently
Compare the meanings
  • Scrutinizing is a process of checking; cogent describes the quality of the resulting reasoning. Confidence alone does not make an argument cogent.
